# Shared Design Guidelines

Version 1.1 · 30 September 2026

## Intent

Build interfaces with clear structure, a recognisable identity and room for their content to breathe. Aim for the discipline of a well-edited publication: every element has a purpose, and the layout makes relationships easy to understand.

Use these principles across projects while preserving each project's purpose, palette and typography. LocalCanon can feel like a cultural magazine; a working dashboard should still behave like a working dashboard.

## 1. Start with the content

Before choosing components, identify what the reader needs to understand or do. Group related material, establish its order and choose the simplest suitable representation.

| Content or task | Starting representation |
| --- | --- |
| Categories or destinations | Simple list or navigation group |
| Steps in a process | Ordered list |
| Records that need comparison | Table with useful columns |
| Facts about one object | Label/value pairs |
| A story or explanation | Reading column with a clear heading |
| A lead editorial feature | Image, headline and supporting text in a deliberate composition |
| A photographic collection | Gallery with captions and credits |
| Independent items with separate actions | Cards, when their boundaries help selection or scanning |
| A tool or workflow | A working surface organised around the task |

Do not turn every group into a card. A list should remain a list unless another format helps the reader. Do not add icons, badges or illustrations solely to fill space.

## 2. Make relationships visible

Establish hierarchy through headings, type size, alignment, proximity and spacing first. Related items share an alignment and sit closer together; a new section receives more space and a clear heading.

Use a consistent grid. Allow deliberate asymmetry when one item deserves greater prominence. Avoid giving every section, image and heading equal visual weight.

The first screen should make the page's subject, main action and next section easy to identify. Keep the reading order coherent when columns stack on a small screen.

## 3. Avoid decorative one-sided strokes

Do not use a coloured left, top, right or bottom border as the default treatment for headings, cards, callouts or sections. Do not repeat a short accent line above each heading or alternate its colour to make otherwise identical groups look distinct.

Solve the hierarchy with structure and typography. Add a surface change only when it communicates a real distinction.

Lines remain appropriate when they have a clear job: restrained row separators, form-field boundaries, navigation selection, links, focus outlines and controls. A status indicator may also be appropriate when it has a consistent meaning and a text label. This rule targets decoration, not functional clarity.

## 4. Use surfaces selectively

Most reading areas should share a calm base surface. A change of background can mark a change of role: an editorial sidebar, a featured story, a working area or a genuinely important notice.

Avoid a coloured panel for every section. Several consecutive sections can belong on the same surface, separated by spacing and headings. Prefer a small number of meaningful surface changes over a sequence of alternating pastel blocks.

Use background photography where the image carries context or atmosphere, such as a hero or an editorial transition. Keep dense text on a readable surface. Check cropping, contrast and legibility at different screen sizes.

## 5. Let imagery supply texture

Choose photographs for their relationship to the content. Documentary detail, people, materials, everyday activity and imperfect light can make a disciplined layout feel organic.

Use an analogue film treatment sparingly and consistently when it fits the project. Preserve original files, avoid obscuring meaningful details and keep captions and credits accessible.

Do not substitute decorative background images for clear section structure. Do not use a location photograph to imply an interview, contributor or verified activity that it does not document.

## 6. Give colour a job

Each project defines its own colour roles: base surface, body text, primary identity, supporting accent and featured emphasis. Repeat those meanings consistently.

Keep bright accents concentrated where they direct attention. Preserve enough neutral space for an accent to stand out. Do not rely on colour alone to explain status, selection or priority.

For LocalCanon: mostly white, soft off-black text, navy as the primary colour, orange and yellow as supporting accents, and red for featured emphasis. The palette is specific to LocalCanon; other projects retain their own identities.

## 7. Use type as structure

Assign clear roles to page titles, section headings, item headings, body copy and captions. Use a small, consistent set of type styles. A second typeface should add a useful voice or distinction.

Keep body text comfortable to read and line lengths controlled. Small metadata should remain legible. Avoid oversized headings everywhere, excessive uppercase labels and decorative labels that repeat the heading beneath them.

## 8. Keep interaction explicit

Links should describe their destination; buttons should describe their action. Make interactive elements recognisable without depending on hover. Preserve visible keyboard focus and adequate contrast.

Choose controls for the task rather than for their appearance. A carousel suits optional browsing; material that needs side-by-side comparison should remain visible together.

Describe missing content honestly. Empty states should explain what is missing and, where appropriate, offer a next action. Do not pad the interface with invented statistics, quotes, people or activity.

## LocalCanon application

The central moodboard is the visual reference for editorial composition: a strong photographic opening, a compact theme strip, a lead feature and a clearly distinct community sidebar. Borrow its hierarchy and rhythm while retaining the current navy-led palette.

Applied to the LocalCanon prototype on 30 September 2026:

- The translucent hero context panel uses its placement and surface to establish its role; the orange top stroke is removed.
- Business categories form a simple list under one heading. The directory update sits alongside it on wide screens and below it on narrow screens.
- The directory roadmap uses a heading and short explanation, without a decorative red left stroke.
- The listening room's traditional and contemporary sections use headings, spacing and content. Featured red is reserved for the featured-song label.
- Decorative masthead, footer and empty-state accent strokes are removed. Restrained archive-row separators and functional navigation and focus indicators remain.

## Review and adoption

Before completing an interface change, check:

- Does the format suit the content and task?
- Can the hierarchy be understood without decorative borders?
- Does every surface change explain a meaningful difference?
- Does imagery add context, with accurate captions and attribution?
- Are prominence and spacing deliberate rather than uniform everywhere?
- Do reading order, contrast, keyboard focus and navigation work on narrow screens?
- Does the result retain this project's identity?

Keep a copy of this guide in each project's design documentation and reference it from that repository's contributor instructions. Project-specific design systems supply the concrete fonts, colours and component details. Adopt the guide incrementally during relevant design work; do not assume it has automatically been applied to every existing project.
